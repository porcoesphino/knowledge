# Planet Money Summer School

## Intro Econ 1: Choices & Dating

--- --- ---
***<!--c1::-->Opportunity cost*** - The value of the next best thing you're giving
up. Like if you stay up late watching Netflix then the opportunity cost might be
feeling energized and productive the next morning.
--- ---
--- --- ---
***<!--c1::-->Marginal utility*** - The cost/benefit of an extra unit of something.
Like when you think four chicken nuggets is a good dinner for you but for the same
price you can get six and if you do there is also a cost to your health for buying
those extra two, but if you are staving then those two chick nuggets may be life-and-death.
--- ---
--- --- ---
***<!--c1::-->Sunk cost*** - Costs that can't be reversed. Like a non-refundable
concert ticket on the day your baby is being born.
--- ---
--- --- ---
***<!--c1::-->Market equilibrium*** - When the supply and demand are balanced. Like
with Uber surge pricing where drivers are offered more money as customers are willing
to pay more until a balance is reached.
--- ---

## Intro Econ 2: Markets and Pickles

--- --- ---
***<!--c1::-->[Local knowledge
problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_knowledge_problem)*** - The argument
that the information required for rational economic planning is distributed among
individual actors so some of it is missing, or costly to obtain, for a central authority.
--- ---
--- --- ---
***<!--c1::-->[Gains from
trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gains_from_trade)*** - The net benefits to
from being allowed an increase in voluntary trading with each other since trades
shuffle who gets what and by balancing needs should increase overall utility.
A market can be a useful tool for this.
--- ---
--- --- ---
The ***<!--c1::-->market*** balances out broad needs. The top of the organisation
doesn't have enough information but a ***<!--c1::-->market*** is self balancing.
Information is too disperse and ***<!--c2::-->prices*** act as a mechanism to
redistribute this information.
--- ---
--- --- ---
Market failures

- Market ***<!--c1::-->power*** can undermine competitive pressures. Like Amazon
being a monopoly that can set prices that benefit them more.
- When there is low trust or information asymmetry, like buying a car from a used
car salesman then product quality may ***<!--c2::-->degrade***.
(See [The Market for Lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Market_for_Lemons))
- When trading in cash is ***<!--c2::-->disgusting / unethical***. An example of
this is organ trading for cash that would have problematic effects for some
groups, like the poor.

--- ---
A ***<!--c1::-->market economy*** is an economic system where the production of
goods is guided by supply and demand.
--- ---
--- --- ---
A ***<!--c1::-->planned economy*** is a type of economic system that has centralised
economy-wide economic and production plans.

--- ---
--- --- ---
A ***<!--c1::-->mixed economy*** includes attributes of a market economy and
attributes of a planned economy.
--- ---
